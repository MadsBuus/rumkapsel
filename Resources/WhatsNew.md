## What's new in 0.43

- The Kingdom keeps what it can between redraws, since it was giving back everything the floor work won. A redraw of a forty-office castle cost 1,447ms against Classic's 116ms, and measuring it a piece at a time found the whole of it in one place: the ground. With the world left out the same redraw is 86ms, which is quicker than Classic; everything else this look draws is already cheap.
- The Kingdom says what it draws, since the gate now asks. It takes every piece a look may hand over but the floor lettering, which it does without: a ward is known by its banner. Whether a theme letters its floor is part of what it claims rather than a name checked in the test
- A ward is a room you can read: boarded floors inside, cobbles on the street, its own wall round it and its number on its banner. A look is told what an office is known by — its issue, whoever is in it, and the name that would have been written — so it can mark the office some other way; the Kingdom flies the number, since a name will not fit on a banner and a number will, with the occupant under it, and the banner carries the office's own name for the pointer, so it is hovered and clicked like the ward it stands over.
- The scroll hangs the way the slab does. It carried the tablet's numbers from before they were corrected: a positive pitch drops the far edge and turns the writing away, so a look that brings its own tool reproduced a bug the classic piece no longer has
- The castle's people are its own, so the twelve kit figures go with them: twenty thousand lines of models nobody draws, and with them the last steel drums, which stood at the caravan gate and in the tavern where the pirate kit's barrels belong. The gallery shows what the look draws rather than the scene's own piece, and gains a tile for the pieces a look may now hand over: the handcart, the lectern and a stack of bound chests
- Round two: what a look may not draw, it may now. Four things a theme could not touch were hardcoded in the scene, so they are hooks rather than workarounds: the console a pallet is ordered at, the crate work rides in, what stands round a release that is held, and the float that carries goods; and a look may now say that nothing is lettered on its floor, and carry the names itself.
- A keep worth finding, a harbour with water in it, and the kits cut back to what the castle uses. The center is built rather than borrowed: a square donjon on a stone motte, four turrets carried past its battlements, tall lights on every face, a great door at its foot and the banner at the top, where it was a small tower that could have stood anywhere.
- The Kingdom becomes a castle, and what is shipped sails. The curtain wall is run along the station's own outline, a stretch of rampart on every edge that faces open ground with towers at corners well apart, so the castle's shape is the floor plan's and it gains a new stretch whenever an office opens; a straight run is one stone rather than one a tile, and the stones are baked into a geometry of their own, since flattenedClone loses where pieces stand when they were never in a scene. Offices are flagstoned wards with the house's banner at the wall where they were tilled fields, and hallways are cobbled.
- Kenney Kingdom: each station a harbour village in a world of its own
- The Cape is only known as far as the station reaches. Past that the ground fades into fog in the colour of the sky — veils laid one over the next as the known world runs out, the last of them solid — and the fog draws back as the station grows, a grid square at a time. Nothing is built in it: the coast is cut and the scatter is walked only over the ground somebody can see, so a forty-office redraw that builds the Cape is 342ms where it was 484ms. The edge of what is known wanders rather than following the fleet's rectangle
- One station, always. Sessions could be split across two by a rule — conductor workspaces as work and the rest private, or work by owning organisation — and a fresh install with a conductor folder took that rule by default, with the private station shown, so every new user had two stations whether they wanted them or not. There is one now: every session lands on it unless its repository is kept off.
- The Cape is kept between redraws. Space Center gave back what the floor work won: a redraw of a forty-office fleet cost 861ms against Classic's 117ms, and the whole of it was the ground, cut afresh every time an office opened.
- A gate for what a look promises to draw, and Space Center's own pieces. A look conforms by shape, not by declaration, and Swift has no override keyword to catch a method that has stopped matching its requirement: the old one keeps compiling, nothing calls it, and the default is used instead, which is how every office quietly lost its desk. So each theme now says what it draws differently from the classic station and --look-tests holds it to that, asking the look for every piece that has a classic default and failing where a claim comes back empty. Theme.allCases drives the run, so a theme added without a word about what it draws fails the gate; putting the old signature back makes it fail on the desk.
- Five more pieces a look may draw, and a sign an office is known by. The scene built the console a pallet is ordered at, the crate work rides in, the barrier round a release that is held and the float that carries goods, and no theme could touch any of them; each is a hook now, with the classic piece as the default, so a look says only what it draws differently. A look may also say that nothing is lettered on its floor, in which case it carries the names itself, and an office's dressing is given what that office is known by — its issue number, whoever is working in it, and the name that would have been written — and is named for the office, so whatever a look puts there is hovered and clicked like the ward it stands over.
